Daily protein

At least 112 g a day, aim 120–140 g. Calories about what you burn (~2,300–2,500 kcal). Mess food gives ~30 g; the rest you add on purpose.

A day that hits ~120 g, no kitchen: milk at breakfast, whey after the gym, 50 g soaked soya chunks in lunch dal, curd at lunch and dinner, roasted chana and peanuts as snacks, extra dal at both meals. Paneer days are a bonus. (If you eat eggs, 4 boiled eggs add 24 g.)

Tuesday (fast): milk + semiyan gives ~20–25 g. That's fine: one low day a week barely matters. If your fast allows it, a scoop of whey in the milk brings it to ~45 g.

Digestion

Dry sabzi over gravy, leave the oil layer behind. Curd or buttermilk daily. Spread protein over 3–5 feeds. Walk 10 min after meals. ~3 L water. Pain, or trouble that always follows certain foods → see a doctor.

Sleep

7½–8 hours. Short sleep costs muscle and keeps fat on, even with perfect training.

Supplements

Creatine monohydrate, 3–5 g every day

The best-supported supplement there is (ISSN 2017, 200+ studies). Vegetarian diets start with lower stores, so you may gain more from it. Any time of day, with water. Plain monohydrate, nothing fancy. Expect ~1 kg of water weight in the first weeks; that's muscle water, not fat.

Whey

Not magic, just milk protein in powder form, so it is vegetarian. On a vegetarian hostel diet it's the easiest way to close the gap; 1 scoop a day is usually enough.

Vitamin B12 check

Vegetarians in India are commonly low in B12. Cheap blood test; if low, a doctor will give you a supplement. Low B12 means tiredness that no plan can train through.

Skip

BCAAs, fat burners, testosterone boosters, 'mass gainers'. None are worth your money.
