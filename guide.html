How Training Works

Instagram reels show exercises. What actually makes a plan work is a handful of rules about how much, how hard, how often and how you add to it. Here they are, each with what it means for your week.

01

Progressive overload

Your muscles adapt to exactly what you ask of them, then stop. To keep growing you have to ask a bit more over time: more reps, then more weight. If you lift the same 10 kg for 10 reps forever, your body has no reason to change.

Example: your Romanian deadlift (numbers are illustrative). Small steps, not every week. When you stall, an easy week lets fatigue clear so you come back stronger.

The method you'll use: double progression

Every exercise in your plan has a rep range, like 3 × 12–15. Keep the same weight and try to beat your reps each week. Once every set hits the top of the range, go up to the next weight. Your reps drop back to the bottom of the range, and you climb again.

Lateral raise, 3 × 12–15. A real week-1 log might look like 12, 11, 10. That's fine: the bottom of the range on the first set is enough. Move up only when you get 15, 15, 15.

Your job

Log every lift in the "Last:" box on the plan (e.g. 40 × 10,9,8). Next week, beat it by one rep somewhere. That box is progressive overload. Without it you're guessing.

02

How hard: stop 1–2 reps short

A set only builds muscle if it's hard. The last few reps, when the bar slows down, do most of the work. A 2024 meta-analysis found muscle growth rises the closer you go to failure. Strength gains, though, barely depended on it. Going all the way to failure on every set mostly adds fatigue.

"2 reps in reserve": you stop when you're sure you could do 2 more with clean form, but not 4.

  • Big lifts (squat, deadlift, bench): 2 in reserve. Failing these is risky and very tiring.
  • Small moves (curls, lateral raises, push-ups): 0–1 in reserve on the last set is fine.
  • Beginner warning: new lifters nearly always underestimate what they can do. Once a month, take the last set of a safe exercise (like a machine or push-ups) to true failure. That teaches you where "2 left" really is.
03

How much: sets per muscle per week

The best single number to count is hard sets per muscle per week. More sets give more growth, but every extra set adds a little less than the one before. Even 4 sets a week builds muscle. You'll often hear "10–20 sets is optimal". Greg Nuckols (Stronger By Science) traced that number back: it came from how one meta-analysis grouped its studies, and there was "never a long period of time where there was a strong empirical case" for it. His practical advice: about 10 sets a week is a good starting point, then add sets if you're recovering and progress stalls.

How your plan uses this

Back and side delts get the most (12 and 10 sets) because your goal is a V-taper. Everything else starts at 5–9 sets, which is plenty for a beginner. From week 6, if you're recovering well and lifts keep rising, the plan adds sets to the muscles you care about most.

04

How often: each muscle twice a week

Once your weekly sets are matched, how you spread them out barely matters for growth. Twice a week is still the smart default. Doing 20 sets of chest in one session means the last 10 are junk, because you're too tired to make them hard. Split across two days, every set is good quality.

Rule of thumb

No more than about 8–10 hard sets for one muscle in one session. If you need more, spread them over another day.

05

Splits: push, pull, legs and the rest

A split is just how you divide the body across the week. None is magic. A 2024 meta-analysis compared splits with full-body training at equal weekly sets and found no real difference in muscle or strength. Pick the one that fits your week and that you'll actually turn up for.

Why yours is full body

Your week decides it. Gym and pool are both at 4 PM, so you can only lift on Mon, Tue, Thu and Sat, and Tuesday is your fast day. Squatting and deadlifting after ~20 hours without food is asking for dizziness, so the heavy work goes into three full-body days (Mon, Thu, Sat), each muscle trained 2–3 times a week. Full body is also what Eric Helms (PhD, natural pro bodybuilder) offers beginners as his 3-day option, and the 2024 meta-analysis above found it builds as much muscle as a split at the same weekly sets. Tuesday is a short, optional machine session for shoulders and arms.

06

Reps, weight and rest

  • Any rep range from about 5 to 30 builds muscle, as long as the set ends close to failure. Heavy weights (3–8 reps) build strength best. That's why your main lifts sit at 6–10 reps and the small moves at 12–15.
  • Rest longer than you think. Rest that's too short makes the next set weaker, and you lose reps. Research finds a small benefit to resting over 60 s, and little difference beyond 90 s.
    → Big lifts: 2–3 min. Small moves: 60–90 s. Ab circuits: as written.
  • Form before weight. A rep only counts if it goes through the full range shown in the GIF. Adding weight by cutting the range short is fake progress.
07

Recovery: where growth actually happens

Training is the signal; muscle is built in the 24–72 hours after, if you give it materials and sleep.

08

Why ab exercises won't burn belly fat

Spot reduction, burning fat where you exercise, doesn't happen. A 2021 meta-analysis of studies that trained only one limb found the trained and untrained limbs lost the same fat. Your belly fat leaves when your overall body fat drops, from food and total training.

Ab training still matters: it makes the muscles thicker, so they show sooner once the fat is gone. That's why your plan has ab work three days a week, and why the reels promising "no belly in 30 days with 100 crunches" were left out.

09

The whole thing on one card

Every session
  • Warm up, then do the big lift first, while you're fresh.
  • Every set ends 1–2 reps from failure.
  • Log weight × reps. Beat last week by a rep or a little weight.
  • Rest 2–3 min on big lifts, 60–90 s on small ones.
Every week
  • About 10 hard sets per muscle to start, each muscle trained twice; add sets only when you're recovering and stuck.
  • Protein ≥ 112 g a day, 7½+ h of sleep.
  • Weigh yourself every morning and watch the weekly average, not the daily number.
Every 2 weeks

Check lifts, waist and weight average against the rules on your plan page, and adjust only what the rules say. Photos every 4 weeks in the same light.
