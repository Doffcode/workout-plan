How This Plan Was Made

Version 1 was built from 14 reels your feed happened to show you. Nobody checked whether those creators knew what they were doing, or whether their claims were true. Version 2 was built the other way round: first find people worth trusting, then check what they say against the research, then check every exercise video.

STEP 1

Who to trust

Followers counted for nothing. To be included, a person had to pass all four:

  1. A record you can check. I counted each person's papers in PubMed (the medical research database), or required a clinical licence for form and injury advice.
  2. Shows the evidence. Cites studies and says how strong they are.
  3. Admits uncertainty. Says "we don't know" and has publicly changed their mind.
  4. No miracle claims, and the advice isn't just a way to sell something.
The groupthink trap you worried about

Many top "evidence-based" names write papers and podcasts together, so them agreeing isn't independent. To guard against that, I added two opposing schools (Steele & Fisher, Beardsley) and used published meta-analyses as the tie-breaker, not headcount.

STEP 4

Technique: where the coaches disagreed

Every cue on your plan is one that at least two independent sources gave (RP's many videos count as one source). When they disagreed:

  • Romanian deadlift depth. Nippard and Squat University: stop around mid-shin; going to the floor makes most people round their lower back. RP's demo went to the floor, so your RDL GIF now comes from Nippard's video instead.
  • Push-up hips. RP likes them slightly high; Squat University wants a straight line. The plan uses the straight line.
  • Pull-up shoulder blades. Nippard cues "shoulders down first"; RP says don't worry. Both agree on what matters: full hang, chin over the bar, no swinging.
  • Squat toe angle. RP ~45°, Nippard 15–30°, Squat University closer to straight. The plan says "slightly out, whatever lets your knees follow your toes".
  • Grip widths, bench angles, elbow angles: every coach said "try a few and keep what you feel without joint pain". The plan says the same instead of inventing a rule.
STEP 5

Your 14 reels, audited

I checked all 24 exercise clips frame by frame against that technique.

  • 1 unsafe: the trap-bar deadlift was done with a rounded back. Replaced.
  • 1 with a fault: hips sagging in the brick push-ups. Replaced.
  • 3 badly cut: clips that mixed two different exercises. Dropped.
  • 6 with false promises: "fix your belly bulge", "fix rounded shoulders". The moves are kept as optional Sunday mobility, without the promise.
  • Bodyweight moves were fine, just shaky footage. Every gym-lift demo now comes from a vetted coach.
THE QUESTION

Will it work 99 times out of 100?

Honest answer: nobody can promise that for any fixed plan, and anyone who does is the kind of source this process filters out. Research shows people respond very differently: one study found the differences between people were about 40× bigger than the effect of tweaking the program. What studies also show: when strength, size and fitness are all measured, practically everyone improves at something, and longer training means more people respond.

So the plan doesn't rely on being perfect on day one. It has three safeguards:

  1. Built only from what is solid, so it works for the average beginner.
  2. You measure yourself (lifts, waist, weight average) every 2 weeks.
  3. Pre-written rules tell you exactly what to change when a number stalls. That turns "will it work for me?" into "it adjusts until it does", and you never need a new plan.

If you train consistently, hit your protein and sleep, and follow the rules, the realistic expectation is roughly 1–2 kg of muscle and 30–50% stronger lifts in the first 8–12 weeks.

FROM YOUR REELS

What your reels got wrong

  • Stretch routines that 'fix your belly bulge' or 'fix rounded shoulders'
    A 2024 review of 23 trials: stretching did not change posture at all. Strength training did. Those mobility moves are pleasant, so they're kept as an optional Sunday routine, minus the promise.
  • 100 squats / knee raises a day for 'no belly in a month'
    Fat doesn't come off the spot you exercise (2021 meta-analysis: zero effect). Your belly shrinks with overall fat loss.
  • 50 kneeling side reaches a day; 50 × 3 burpees
    Spot reduction again, and a conditioning stunt. Your three swims already cover conditioning.
  • 'Your body can only use ~30 g of protein per meal'
    Not true: a 2023 study fed 100 g at once and muscle building kept going for 12 hours. Spread protein out because it's easier on your stomach, not because the rest is wasted.
  • The trap-bar deadlift clip
    Great story, but the lift in the clip is done with a rounded back. Not a technique demo. Replaced with a coach's demo.
